# AI Risk Part 3

## AI race

Competition could push nations and corporations to rush
Al development, relinquishing control to these systems

Cyberwarfare, autonomous weapons, automate human labor -> mass unemployment and dependence on Al systems

## AI race : Military

Low-cost automated weapons, such as drone swarms outfitted with explosives, could autonomously hunt human targets with high
precision, performing lethal operations for both militaries and terrorist groups and lowering the barriers to large-scale violence.

## AI race : Corporate

As Als automate increasingly many tasks, the economy may become largely run by Als. Eventually, this could lead to human **enfeeblement** and dependence on Als for basic needs.

enfeeblement - weak  

## AI race : Solutions

* Safety regulations: self regulation of companies(SRO), competitive advantage for safety oriented companies  
* Data documentation: transparency & accountability
* Meaningful human oversight: human supervision
* AI for cyber defense: anomaly detection
International coordination: standards for AI development, robust verification & enforcement
* Public control of general-purpose AIs

## Organizational risks

* Organizations developing advanced Al cause catastrophic accidents; profits over safety
* AIs could be accidentally leaked to the public or stolen by malicious actors, and organizations could fail to properly invest in safety research
* New capabilities can emerge quickly and unpredictably during training, such that dangerous milestones may be crossed without our knowing

![alt text](image-67.png)

The Swiss cheese model shows how technical factors can improve organizational safety. Multiple layers of defense compensate for each other's individual weaknesses, leading to
a low overall level of risk.

### Organizational risks : Solutions

* Red teaming
* Prove safety
* Deployment
* Publication reviews
* Response plans
* Risk management: Employ a **chief risk officer** and an internal audit team for risk management.
* Processes for important decisions: Make sure Al training or deployment decisions involve the chief risk officer and other key stakeholders, ensuring executive accountability.

## Rouge AIs : power seeking

It can be instrumentally rational for Als to engage in self-preservation. Loss of control over such systems could be hard to recover from.

## Rouge AIs : Deception

Various resources, such as money and computing power, can sometimes be instrumentally rational to seek. Als which can capably pursue goals may take intermediate steps to gain power
and resources.

### Rouge AIs : Solutions

Als should not be deployed in high-risk settings, such as by autonomously pursuing open-ended goals or overseeing critical infrastructure, **unless proven safe.**

Need to advance Al safety research in areas such as adversarial robustness, model honesty,
transparency, and removing undesired capabilities.

## A Notional Decomposition of Risk

Risk = Vulnerability x Hazard Exposure x Hazard

**Vulnerability:** a factor or process that increases susceptibility to the damaging effects
of hazards

**Exposure:** extent to which elements (e.g., people, property, systems) are subjected
or exposed to hazards

**Hazard:** a source of danger with the potential to harm
